There's a myth that tattooing starts when the machine turns on. For custom work,
that's the easy part — and the last part. The real artistry happens earlier, in a
sketchbook, on a tablet, and in a stencil, where a great tattoo is mostly *drawn*
before a needle ever touches skin. Here's the craft most clients never see.

## It starts as drawing — real drawing

The best tattoo artists are, first, **fine artists**. They understand composition,
value (the range from light to dark), color theory, and line economy — the same
fundamentals you'd learn in an art-school foundations class. A custom piece often
begins with reference gathering and rough thumbnails, long before anything looks
like the final tattoo.

## Designing for a moving canvas

This is what separates a tattoo artist from an illustrator: skin isn't flat paper.
A great custom design has **flow** — it's drawn to wrap the muscle and follow the
body, so it looks right when the arm bends and the shoulder rolls. A sleeve is
composed around the limb, not just slapped onto it. Getting that flow right is
pure spatial craft, and it's the difference between a collection of tattoos and a
single cohesive piece.

## Value, contrast, and the squint test

Artists build a piece in values first — where's the deepest black, where's the
skin left bare for highlight. A common pro move: squint at the design until detail
disappears and only the light-and-dark shapes remain. If it reads as a strong
image when blurred, it'll read across a crowded room and survive the
[softening that comes with age](/blog/fine-line-vs-bold-traditional-aging/).

## The stencil — the bridge

The finished drawing becomes a stencil, placed and re-placed on the skin until the
position and flow are exactly right. Only then does the tattooing — the
[lining, shading, and packing](/blog/tattoo-techniques-single-needle-whip-shading-stippling/)
— begin. The needle is just the final tool that makes a finished drawing permanent.

## Custom vs. flash

None of this knocks flash — bold, ready-to-go designs off the wall are a proud
tradition and perfect for a lot of tattoos. But when you commission **custom**,
you're paying for the drawing hours and the design mind as much as the needle
time. That artistry is exactly what we look for when we
[crown the best in a city](/blog/how-we-crown-the-best-tattoo-artist/).
