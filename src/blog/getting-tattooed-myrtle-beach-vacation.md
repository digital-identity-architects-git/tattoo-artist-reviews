A tattoo is the rare souvenir that doesn't end up in a junk drawer. Done right, it
outlasts every t-shirt and shot glass you'll buy on the boardwalk. Done wrong, on
a whim, at the last open shop at midnight — it's the one you spend next summer
getting [covered up](/blog/myrtle-beach-tattoo-scene-field-guide/). Here's how to
land on the right side of that line.

## Walk-in or appointment?

Both work in a vacation town — but for different jobs.

- **Walk in** for small, classic, flash-style pieces: a bold-traditional anchor, a
  script word, a palm-sized design off the wall. Fast, fun, and exactly what a lot
  of Myrtle Beach shops are built to do well.
- **Book ahead** for anything custom, large, or detailed. A good artist's calendar
  fills up, especially in summer. If you want a specific person's hands on a real
  piece, email or DM before your trip — the work in our
  [techniques guide](/blog/tattoo-techniques-single-needle-whip-shading-stippling/)
  isn't a same-day, walk-up job.

## Vet the shop in five minutes

Tourist towns have great shops and tourist traps side by side. Read the work, not
the window. Ask for **healed** photos, glance at the station for single-use
cartridges and barrier film (here's [what clean looks like](/blog/whats-in-the-tray-tattoo-station-tour/)),
and trust your gut. A real artist will happily talk you through their setup.

## The aftercare nobody mentions on the way out

This is the vacation-specific part, and it's where beach trips go wrong:

1. **No ocean, no pool — period.** A fresh tattoo is an open wound. Saltwater and
   chlorine are an infection risk and will pull color. Wait at least two weeks.
2. **Cover it from the sun.** A sunburn on fresh ink is brutal and fades the
   tattoo. Loose clothing beats sunscreen on a healing piece.
3. **Sand is the enemy.** Keep it clean and covered on the beach.

Get a tattoo on day one of the trip and you're choosing ink over the ocean for the
rest of the week. Plan it for the **end** of your stay instead.

## Tip your artist

Tattooing is skilled, physical work. If you love the result, 15–20% is standard.
