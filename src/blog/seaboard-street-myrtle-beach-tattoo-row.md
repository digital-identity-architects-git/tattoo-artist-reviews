Every great tattoo town has a street. In Myrtle Beach, it's **Seaboard Street** —
a low-slung industrial stretch tucked off Highway 17 near Coastal Grand Mall that
doesn't look like much until you start counting the studios.

This is part of our [Myrtle Beach field guide](/blog/myrtle-beach-tattoo-scene-field-guide/).
If you want to understand the Grand Strand's ink, you start here.

## Why the talent clustered

Tattoo districts form the way restaurant rows do: artists want to be where the
foot traffic and the other good artists already are. Seaboard sits a quick drive
from the boardwalk and the mall, which means a steady flow of walk-ins, but it's
just off the tourist drag — rent that lets a serious studio build a real space
instead of a souvenir counter. The result is a strip where shops compete on craft,
not just on who has the brightest sign facing the highway.

## Anchors of the row

Elite Ink Tattoos sits at 914 Seaboard Street with a wall of "Best of the Beach"
awards and a deep bench of artists working across styles. A few doors down, Aces
High Tattoo at 704 Seaboard has artists who've been tattooing in Myrtle Beach
since the '90s — the kind of tenure that means healed work you can actually go
look at. Between them and the shops in the immediate orbit, you can stand in one
parking lot and be within walking distance of decades of combined experience.

We're not crowning anyone yet — that's the whole point of doing the homework
first. But if you're building a Myrtle Beach tattoo plan, Seaboard Street is the
most efficient square mile to start your research.

## How to walk the row

Don't shop on price alone — read the work. Ask to see **healed** photos, not just
fresh ones, and look at the station before you look at the flash on the wall. Our
guides on [what a clean setup looks like](/blog/whats-in-the-tray-tattoo-station-tour/)
and [the machines doing the work](/blog/coil-vs-rotary-vs-wireless-tattoo-machines/)
will tell you more about a shop in five minutes than any review site.
