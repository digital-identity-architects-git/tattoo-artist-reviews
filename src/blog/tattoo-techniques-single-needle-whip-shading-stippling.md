Two artists can run the identical machine and produce work that looks like it came
from different planets. The difference is **technique** — how the needle moves,
how deep it goes, how the hand controls speed and angle. Here's a plain-English
tour of the marks behind the styles, so you can actually read a portfolio instead
of just liking it.

## Lining

The skeleton of most tattoos. A liner lays down the outline in a single confident
pass — and a single pass is the goal, because going over a line twice is what
causes blowouts and uneven weight. Clean, consistent lining is the first thing we
look at when we [judge an artist's work](/blog/how-we-crown-the-best-tattoo-artist/),
because nothing hides a wobble.

## Whip shading

A flicking, "whipping" motion that lifts the needle as it moves, leaving a
gradient that fades from dark to light. It's how artists get soft, smoky shadow
without hard edges — think the haze around a black-and-grey skull. Fast, fluid,
and unforgiving; the transition either looks like smoke or looks like mud.

## Stippling (dotwork)

Shading built entirely from dots. Pack them tight for shadow, space them out for
light. Dotwork ages beautifully and gives the etched, old-engraving look you see
in geometric and blackwork pieces — but it's slow, meditative work that rewards
patience over flash.

## Color packing

Saturating an area with solid, even color so there are no patchy "holidays" where
ink didn't take. Good packing is why a bold traditional tattoo still looks vivid
years later — and why [bold styles tend to age so well](/blog/fine-line-vs-bold-traditional-aging/).

## Single-needle

Exactly what it sounds like: one needle, for the finest possible line. It's the
backbone of micro and fine-line work — gorgeous, delicate, and the least forgiving
technique on this list. A true single-needle specialist is worth seeking out.

## Why this matters when you book

When an artist's portfolio shows command of *several* of these — crisp lines,
smooth whip-shaded gradients, clean color packing — you're looking at range, and
range is a hallmark of the best in any city. The [machine they use](/blog/coil-vs-rotary-vs-wireless-tattoo-machines/)
matters less than the hand driving it.
