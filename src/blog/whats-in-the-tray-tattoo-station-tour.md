Before you look at an artist's flash, look at their **station**. The little tray
beside the chair is the most honest portfolio in the shop — it tells you, in about
five seconds, whether the person about to put a permanent wound on your body
respects the craft and your health. Here's how to read it.

## The setup, piece by piece

- **Barrier film (the blue plastic wrap).** A clean station has everything the
  artist touches — machine, clip cord, spray bottles, even the chair — wrapped in
  fresh plastic that gets thrown out and replaced for every client. This is the
  single biggest tell of a pro.
- **Single-use cartridges/needles.** Needles should come out of a **sealed, sterile
  pouch in front of you** and go in the sharps bin when they're done. Never reused,
  ever, between clients.
- **Ink caps.** Small disposable cups of ink, poured fresh for your session and
  tossed after. The ink bottle never touches your skin or your wound.
- **Green soap & distilled water.** The mild soap used to clean and stencil the
  skin and wipe during the tattoo.
- **Gloves.** Fresh, and re-changed if the artist steps away and touches anything
  outside the sterile field.
- **An autoclave (in back).** The machine that sterilizes any reusable tubes or
  grips. Reputable shops have one and will tell you about it.

## How to read a station like a pro

Walk in and glance at the tray. Is everything wrapped? Are the needles still in
sealed packaging? Is the floor and counter clean, the sharps container proper and
not overflowing? A real artist is *proud* of their setup and happy to walk you
through it. Hesitation or a messy, unwrapped station is your cue to leave.

## Why we weigh this so heavily

Cleanliness isn't separate from skill — it *is* skill. Discipline at the station
is the same discipline that produces clean lines and well-healed work. It's a core
part of [how we crown an artist](/blog/how-we-crown-the-best-tattoo-artist/), and
it's the easiest thing for *you* to check before you ever sit down — whether you're
on [Seaboard Street](/blog/seaboard-street-myrtle-beach-tattoo-row/) or anywhere
else.
