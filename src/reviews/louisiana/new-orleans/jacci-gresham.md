You can't tell the story of tattooing in New Orleans — or in America — without Jacci
Gresham. Widely recognized as the first known Black female tattoo artist in the United
States, she didn't just earn a place in the city's scene. She built a large part of it.

## Why she takes the crown

Gresham came to tattooing by an unlikely road: trained in architecture and engineering,
she worked for General Motors in Michigan before moving to New Orleans and opening Aart
Accent in 1976 with her partner Ajit "Ali" Singh. That shop became Louisiana's oldest
continuously operating tattoo business, running roughly 46 years until it closed on Labor
Day 2022. Generations of New Orleanians got their first ink from her chair.

## The style

Across nearly five decades she worked in a full range of custom styles, with a foundation
in bold, old-school American work — clean, confident, made to last. Her influence reaches
far past her own hands; she helped open a door that countless artists have walked through
since.

## Her legacy

In 2011 she was honored as a Pioneer of Female Tattoo Artists. She's featured in the 2012
documentary *Color Outside the Lines* and is the subject of the 2025 picture-book
biography *Make Your Mark*. Aart Accent has closed, but Gresham's mark on New Orleans —
and on the craft itself — is permanent. Follow her legacy on Instagram.
