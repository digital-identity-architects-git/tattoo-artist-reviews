Some artists do tattoos. Kore Flatmo composes them. From PluraBella, the private,
appointment-only studio he opened in Cincinnati in 1999, he's spent decades quietly
building a reputation that pulls collectors in from around the world.

## Why he takes the crown

Flatmo learned the trade in Hollywood in the early '90s, steeped in the Southern
California black-and-grey tradition, and carried that discipline back to Ohio. What
sets him apart is scale and patience: he builds large-format, full-coverage pieces
that read as a single image across the whole body. A full-back samurai charging
through ocean waves isn't a session — it's a relationship measured in dozens of hours.

## The style

He's known for black-and-grey and traditional Japanese-influenced work at a size most
artists never attempt — backs, sleeves, full bodysuits planned as one continuous
composition. He's a fine artist beyond the needle, too; his drawings and paintings
have appeared in books, periodicals, and exhibitions, and that eye shows in every
piece.

## Booking & where to find him

PluraBella runs by appointment only — the way bespoke work should. Reach him through
Instagram or the studio site to start a project. (He's also noted tattooing at Blue
Rock Tattoo in Cincinnati, so confirm the current chair when you book.)
